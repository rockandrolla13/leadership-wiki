## Patterns to Watch For

The following pattern categories come from the source literature and should be tested against case evidence as the wiki accumulates:

### Microstress Patterns (Cross & Dillon)

- **Capacity-draining microstresses** — misalignment with collaborators; uncertainty about reliability; unpredictable authority; high-volume collaborative demand; surges in responsibility.
- **Emotional-reserve depletion** — managing others' wellbeing; confrontational conversations; lack of trust; stress-spreaders; political maneuvering.
- **Identity challenges** — values mismatch; attacks on self-worth; draining family/friend interactions; network disruption.

Test in case pages: are the user's hardest moments more often big events or accumulated small ones?

### Happiness Trap Patterns (McKee)

- **Ambition trap** — winning at all costs; relationships damaged; ethics quietly compromised.
- **"Should" trap** — doing what's expected rather than what's wanted; covering parts of identity at work.
- **Overwork trap** — always-on as identity; degrading decision quality; relationship damage at home.

Test in case pages: which trap is most active for the user? Is there a pattern of one trap activating others?

### Feedback Trigger Patterns (Heen & Stone)

- **Truth triggers** — the user rejects feedback content as wrong before unpacking it.
- **Relationship triggers** — the user can't hear feedback from particular people, however accurate.
- **Identity triggers** — the user's strongest defensive reactions cluster around themes that touch self-concept.

Test in case pages: which trigger is the user's default? What is the cost?

### Leadership Voice Imbalance (Su)

- Over-indexed on voice of *character* (constancy) at the expense of *curiosity* (asking questions).
- Over-indexed on voice of *clarity* (decisive direction) at the expense of *connection* (relationship-building).
- Missing one of the five voices entirely.

Test in case pages: which voices show up most in the user's described interactions? Which are conspicuously absent?

### Question Domain Imbalance (Chevallier/Dalsace/Barsoux)

- Investigative-heavy (always digging into data) but missing speculative ("what if?") or subjective ("what's unsaid?").
- Productive-heavy ("now what?") at the expense of interpretive ("so what?").
- Subjective-allergic — never asking the emotional / unsaid questions.

Test in case pages: what kinds of questions does the user ask in described situations? What kinds are missing?

### Decision-Making Biases (Soll/Milkman/Payne)

- **Narrow future thinking** — single point forecasts, no premortems, no outside view.
- **Narrow objective thinking** — pursues one goal, doesn't generate alternatives, doesn't seek advice on objectives.
- **Narrow option thinking** — frames decisions as binary, doesn't use joint evaluation, doesn't ask "what else could I do?"

Test in case pages: how does the user typically frame decisions? Where does the bias show?

### Hidden-Blocker Patterns (Wilkins)

[[sources/hbr-stop-holding-yourself-back-2025]] — the seven hidden blockers from Muriel Wilkins's research are the most directly diagnostic pattern set for self-imposed leadership limits. Each is a candidate hypothesis to test against case evidence:

- *I need to be involved.* Pattern: micromanagement, bottlenecked decisions, recurring complaint about being overworked while the team is underutilized.
- *I need it done now.* Pattern: chronic false urgency, rushed work that needs rework, team burnout.
- *I know I'm right.* Pattern: meetings end with the user's answer rather than the team's; peers describe the user as not collaborative.
- *I can't make a mistake.* Pattern: indecision on high-visibility decisions; over-consultation; missed windows.
- *If I can do it, so can you.* Pattern: unrealistic standards; team underperforms relative to user's expectations; high turnover of strong performers.
- *I can't say no.* Pattern: chronic overcommitment; poor boundary-setting; team confused about priorities.
- *I don't belong here.* Pattern: under-visible work; declined stretch assignments; impostor-syndrome language in 1:1s.

Test in case pages: which blocker shows up most often in the user's described difficulties? See [[coaching/self-limiting-beliefs]] for the full framework.

### Action-Logic Patterns (Rooke & Torbert)

[[sources/hbr-leadership-vol1]] — the seven action logics name the *frame* through which a leader operates. The patterns to watch for:

- **Expert pattern** (38% of leaders): rules by depth of technical knowledge; defends positions with data; dismisses peers with less expertise. Pattern signal: feedback about not collaborating, despite technical excellence.
- **Achiever pattern** (30%): excels at delivering against goals; inhibits "out of the box" thinking; defends working systems. Pattern signal: stalled at senior management altitude despite strong reviews.
- **Individualist pattern** (10%): unconventional approaches; ignores rules they regard as irrelevant. Pattern signal: irritates colleagues with disregard for established processes.

Test in case pages: which action logic best describes the user's response when their power is challenged? See [[principles/leadership-development-stages]].
