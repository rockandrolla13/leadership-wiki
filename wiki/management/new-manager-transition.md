# New Manager Transition

## Use Case

First 90 days in a management role, or the first 90 days in any new management position (new team, new company, or promotion from IC to manager). The patterns established in this window are hard to change later.

## Diagnosis

You are falling into the New Manager Death Spiral if:
- You are signing up for more work than you can execute
- You are reluctant to delegate because "I can do it better"
- You are withholding context from the team to maintain control
- Your team has stopped bringing you problems because your responses signal you don't want to hear them
- You are telling yourself this state is temporary and will improve on its own

## First Principles

1. **Management is not a promotion — it is a career restart.** The skills that earned you the role are the wrong instincts for the role itself. Individual contributor excellence does not transfer.
2. **You are accountable, not responsible.** You are accountable for outcomes (you can explain what happened and what will be done about it). Your team is responsible for executing. Conflating the two destroys trust.
3. **Let others change your mind.** Your team collectively has more information than you. Listen to that information. When it changes your view, say so explicitly.
4. **The Blue Tape List mindset.** In any new context, everything broken will be visible to you. That hyperawareness is an asset — temporarily. Make a list. Wait a month before acting on it. Many items will resolve or look different with more context.

## Step-by-Step Approach

**Week 1-4: Observe before acting**

1. Schedule 1:1s with every direct report. These are your primary information channels. Listen more than you talk.

2. Start a Blue Tape List — every process, decision, norm, or structure that feels wrong. Do not act yet. Just document.

3. Ask: "What is working well here that I should preserve?" Teams resist new managers who arrive and change everything. Starting with what's working builds credibility for what needs to change.

4. Identify what you do not know about the team, the work, and the context. The depth of your ignorance is proportional to the recency of your arrival.

**Month 2: Understand before acting**

5. Review the Blue Tape List. What has already resolved with context? What still feels important? What do you now understand better?

6. Begin delegating real work — not just tasks, but projects with defined outcomes. Accept that early delegated work will be a B, not an A. That is the correct outcome.

7. Actively look for your instincts leading you wrong. If you find yourself wanting to do the work yourself, pause and ask: "Is this manager behavior or IC behavior?"

**Month 3: Address what remains**

8. Address every Blue Tape List item — fix it, explain why you're not fixing it, or acknowledge you need more time to decide. Don't let items disappear without acknowledgment.

9. Assess the trust level you've built. The signal: are people bringing you problems before they become crises?

## Suggested Language

- **When tempted to take back delegated work:** (Self-prompt) "What would taking this back teach the team about how things work here?"
- **When team brings you a problem you instinctively want to solve:** "What have you already tried? What options are you considering?" (Then coach, not direct)
- **When you've changed your view based on input:** "I've been thinking about what you said about X, and you were right — I've changed my position."
- **When a blue-tape item is going unfixed:** "I've been holding off on addressing [X] until I have more context. Here's what I know so far and when I'll have a better view."

## What To Avoid

- **The New Manager Death Spiral sequence:**
  1. Sign up for too much work
  2. Quality drops; commitments missed
  3. Half-delegate (task without authority)
  4. Team fails on delegated work
  5. Team stops communicating problems
  6. Manager loses visibility
  7. Trust collapses
- **Arriving with a change agenda before you understand the context.** Changes made before you've earned credibility create resistance regardless of whether the changes are correct.
- **Telling yourself the instinct to do the work yourself is leadership.** It is not. It is the thing that prevents leadership.

## Escalation Path

If you are 60 days in and the Death Spiral has already started:
1. Name it to yourself explicitly: "I have been acting as an individual contributor, not a manager."
2. Acknowledge it to your team in general terms: "I've been too hands-on and not trusting you enough with the work. I'm going to change that."
3. Immediately and deliberately give away something that feels important. The discomfort is the point.

## Follow-Up Actions

- At 30 days: review Blue Tape List. Prioritize items. Schedule addresses.
- At 90 days: assess trust levels. Is the team bringing you problems voluntarily?
- Ongoing: track what percentage of your time is spent in management behaviors vs. IC behaviors.

## Sources

Lopp (2020): The **New Manager Death Spiral** chapter synthesizes every wrong instinct a new manager can have into a single cascading failure. The core lesson: the instincts that earned you the promotion are the wrong instincts for the new role.

> "Management is not a promotion, it's a career restart."

Trust is the binding agent that the Death Spiral destroys. The three antidotes Lopp names:

1. **Let others change your mind** (trust through listening).
2. **Build a diverse team** (trust through valuing different perspectives).
3. **Delegate more than is comfortable** (trust through entrusting work).

The **Blue Tape List** (from the Director section) is a separate but complementary tool for managing the hyperawareness of a new context — observe, list, wait, then address. Lopp's point: "everything broken will be visible to you" in the first 30 days, and that hyperawareness is an asset only if you discipline yourself not to act on it immediately.

Lopp on the role transition itself:

> "You are used to having complete visibility and total ownership of your work because that is how it worked in your former individual contributor work life."

That mental model is the thing the New Manager Death Spiral preys on.
