## Core Argument

A compact greatest-hits collection of the HBR articles most often cited as foundational for leadership development. Eight pieces, each a canonical statement of a single discipline: Friedman on Total Leadership (integration across four life domains), Drucker on Managing Oneself, Montgomery on putting leadership back into strategy, Rooke & Torbert on the seven action logics of leader development, Snowden & Boone on the Cynefin decision-making framework, Martin on integrative thinking, Brett/Behfar/Kern on managing multicultural teams, and Buckingham on what great managers do.

## Key Leadership Insights

- **Total Leadership: four domains, mutually reinforcing (Friedman, "Be a Better Leader, Have a Richer Life").** Work, home, community, self — these are not in zero-sum competition; they can be designed to reinforce one another. The Total Leadership method has four steps: REFLECT (clarify what matters), BRAINSTORM POSSIBILITIES (design experiments), CHOOSE EXPERIMENTS (small, designed, time-bound), MEASURE PROGRESS. Nine experiment categories: tracking and reflecting; planning and organizing; rejuvenating and restoring; appreciating and caring; focusing and concentrating; revealing and engaging; time shifting and "re-placing"; delegating and developing; exploring and venturing.
- **Drucker's "Managing Oneself" is canonical and still the best 20-page leadership self-development primer.** Five questions: What are my strengths? How do I perform (am I a reader or a listener; do I work well under pressure or in a structured environment)? What are my values? Where do I belong? What can I contribute? Already in wiki via [[sources/hbr-managing-yourself]]; this collection re-anchors it.
- **Putting Leadership Back into Strategy (Montgomery).** Strategy is *not* a planning exercise; it is a continuous leadership *decision* about purpose, position, and trade-offs. The "strategist as leader" rather than "strategist as analyst." The right question: *Does my organization matter? To whom? Why?*
- **Seven Transformations of Leadership (Rooke & Torbert).** Already in wiki as [[principles/leadership-development-stages]]. Seven action logics — Opportunist, Diplomat, Expert, Achiever, Individualist, Strategist, Alchemist — each with characteristic strengths, blind spots, and developmental pathways. Only ~5% of leaders ever reach Strategist; fewer than 1% reach Alchemist.
- **Cynefin Framework: A Leader's Framework for Decision Making (Snowden & Boone).** Five domains, each with a different decision pattern:
  - **Simple/Obvious:** sense → categorize → respond. Best practice exists. Direct execution.
  - **Complicated:** sense → analyze → respond. Good practice exists. Requires expertise.
  - **Complex:** probe → sense → respond. Emergent practice. Run safe-to-fail experiments.
  - **Chaotic:** act → sense → respond. Novel practice. Stabilize first, then transition to complex.
  - **Disorder:** the unknown domain. Break the situation into the other four.
- **Integrative Thinking (Martin, "How Successful Leaders Think").** Successful leaders are not those who pick the best option from the menu; they *hold opposing models in tension* long enough to design a new option that captures the strengths of both without their limitations. Four-stage cognitive process: salience (what factors are considered), causality (how factors relate), architecture (sequence of decisions), resolution (the integrated solution).
- **Managing Multicultural Teams (Brett, Behfar, Kern).** Already in wiki as [[management/multicultural-teams]]. Four challenges and four interventions:
  - Direct communication vs. indirect → adaptation
  - Trouble with accents and fluency → structural intervention
  - Differing attitudes toward hierarchy → managerial intervention
  - Conflicting norms for decision making → exit
- **What Great Managers Do (Buckingham).** Great managers *discover* what is unique about each person and *capitalize* on it — opposite of treating people uniformly. Three things to know about each direct report: their strengths, their triggers (what activates their strengths), their learning style (analyst, doer, watcher). Counterintuitive insight: "Average managers play checkers; great managers play chess. Checkers pieces are interchangeable; chess pieces each have unique moves."

## Practical Applications

- **Run a Total Leadership 4-step exercise quarterly.** REFLECT (what matters in each of work/home/community/self) → BRAINSTORM (design 3 experiments that benefit ≥2 domains) → CHOOSE 1–2 → MEASURE for 3 months.
- **Apply Cynefin before any major decision.** Which domain is this problem in? Different domain = different process. Most leaders default to "complicated" (call the experts) and miss when a problem is actually "complex" (run experiments) or "chaotic" (stabilize first).
- **For each direct report, write down: strengths, triggers, learning style.** Buckingham's three columns. Update quarterly. Run 1:1s differently for analysts (give them data ahead), doers (give them stretch tasks), watchers (let them shadow you).
- **Use integrative thinking explicitly for "either/or" decisions.** Whenever you're forced to choose between two unsatisfying options, ask: "What is the *third* option that captures the strengths of both?" Hold the tension for 24 hours before deciding.
- **Practice the action-logic developmental move.** Identify your current action logic (the assessment is in Rooke & Torbert's article). The move from Expert → Achiever is the most common (mastery of craft → managing through others). The move from Achiever → Individualist requires losing the security of *systems thinking* in exchange for *systemic doubt.*

## Relevant Quotes

> "Strategy is the work of the leader — it is not a planning exercise to be delegated to a strategy team." — Cynthia Montgomery (paraphrased)

> "Average managers play checkers; great managers play chess. Checkers pieces are interchangeable; chess pieces each have unique moves." — Marcus Buckingham

> "Successful business leaders, we have discovered, share at least one trait: a predisposition and the capacity to hold in their heads two opposing ideas at once. And then, without panicking or simply settling for one alternative or the other, they're able to creatively resolve the tension between those two ideas by generating a new one that contains elements of the others but is superior to both." — Roger Martin

> "Effective decision making requires that leaders adapt their style to the context. Many leaders lead effectively — though usually in only one or two domains (not in all of them) and few, if any, prepare their organizations for diverse contexts." — Snowden & Boone

> "The Total Leadership method is built on three principles: be real, be whole, be innovative. Be real: act with authenticity by clarifying what's important to you. Be whole: act with integrity by recognizing how the different parts of your life affect one another. Be innovative: act with creativity by continually experimenting with how things get done." — Stewart Friedman

## Frameworks Extracted

- **Total Leadership Four-Step Method (Friedman):** REFLECT → BRAINSTORM → CHOOSE EXPERIMENTS → MEASURE. Nine experiment categories cover the design space.
- **Cynefin Framework (Snowden & Boone):** Simple (sense-categorize-respond), Complicated (sense-analyze-respond), Complex (probe-sense-respond), Chaotic (act-sense-respond), Disorder. Each domain requires a different leadership move; misclassifying the domain is the most common decision-making failure.
- **Seven Action Logics (Rooke & Torbert):** Opportunist → Diplomat → Expert → Achiever → Individualist → Strategist → Alchemist. ~5% of leaders reach Strategist; <1% Alchemist.
- **Integrative Thinking Process (Martin):** Salience → Causality → Architecture → Resolution. Hold opposing models in tension; design a third.
- **Buckingham's "Great Manager" Discipline:** for each direct report, know strengths + triggers + learning style. Customize the relationship.
- **Brett/Behfar/Kern Four-Intervention Hierarchy:** adaptation → structural intervention → managerial intervention → exit. Start with the lowest-cost intervention that has a chance.

## Questions This Source Raises

- Cynefin works best when the decision-maker can *recognize* which domain a problem is in. In complex/chaotic problems, that recognition is often only available in hindsight. Are there leading indicators that move a problem from "complicated" to "complex" before it becomes obvious?
- Buckingham's chess-not-checkers stance assumes the manager has the time and information to know each direct report deeply. At spans of control above 7–8, this fidelity becomes impossible. How does the discipline degrade gracefully at scale?
- The seven action logics literature has been criticized for selection bias (it's developed primarily from data on successful Western executives). How does the framework apply in non-Western leadership contexts, or in early-career leaders who have not yet had time to develop a stable logic?
