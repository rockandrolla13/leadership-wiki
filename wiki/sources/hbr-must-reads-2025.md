# HBR's 10 Must Reads 2025

## Core Argument

This year's collection moves past the crisis posture of prior volumes and asks leaders to plan for the long term: how to reskill workforces for an AI-changed economy, how fast to grow without destroying what made you successful, how to sustain empathy without burning out, how to widen talent pools by hiring on skills not credentials, how to remove organizational friction, where DEI goes after the backlash, how to navigate industrial policy and stakeholder capitalism, and how to apply strategic thinking to your own life. The throughline is that durable leadership requires deliberate trade-off thinking — about growth rate, about which obstacles to remove, about which version of "stakeholder capitalism" you actually mean — rather than reactive optimisation.

## Key Leadership Insights

- **Reskilling is a strategic imperative, not an HR project.** Skills now have a half-life under five years; visible CEO and frontline manager ownership of reskilling — built into objectives and review questions like "How have you developed your team?" — is what makes large programmes work.
- **Growth has a sustainable rate set by your non-financial bottlenecks** (talent, culture, operating systems). Pal's Sudden Service paces store openings to the rate at which it can develop owner-operators; Peloton's pandemic over-expansion is the cautionary case. The Rate–Direction–Method (RDM) framework forces all three decisions to be made together.
- **Sustainable empathy is a skill, not a trait.** Distinguish *emotional empathy* (taking on others' feelings) from *empathic concern* (wanting to help). The first burns leaders out; the second drives effectiveness. Tune toward concern.
- **Hiring on skills, not credentials.** Degree-as-proxy is empirically a poor predictor and shrinks the pipeline. A skills-first approach requires an enterprise skills taxonomy, on-ramps (apprenticeships, internships), retrained managers, and hiring in cohorts (not one-at-a-time experiments).
- **"Addition sickness" is a structural drag on most organisations.** The default question is "What can I add?" rather than "What can I get rid of?" Leaders need *good-riddance reviews*, *subtraction games*, the *rule of halves*, and *subtraction specialists* (someone who owns the killing of jargon, meetings, forms).
- **DEI without four freedoms is hollow.** Roberts reframes DEI as the *freedom to be* (authentic), *freedom to become* (grow), *freedom to fade* (step back from spotlight), and *freedom to fail* (recover from mistakes) — all unevenly distributed and all measurable.
- **Activators outperform other rainmaker profiles.** In professional services, the Activator commits to business development as a routine, connects across networks, and creates value through proactive insight — versus Experts, Confidants, Debaters, and Realists, all of which are negatively correlated with performance.
- **Stakeholder capitalism comes in four versions** (instrumental, classic, beneficial, structural) and the failure to distinguish them is the source of board–executive collisions. Leaders should be explicit about which version they are committing to.
- **Use strategic thinking on your own life.** The Strategize Your Life programme adapts corporate strategy steps — define success, articulate purpose, set vision, audit your 168-hour portfolio across six strategic life areas, benchmark, choose, and use OKRs — to make life choices something other than emotion-by-default.

## Practical Applications

- Embed reskilling into manager performance: add "How have you developed your team?" to review templates.
- Before a growth bet, run RDM: is the *rate* set by talent? Does the *direction* fit your real capabilities (not just market size)? Does the *method* (organic / acquire / partner / license) align?
- For empathy, run an internal audit before a hard conversation: "What kind of empathy is this situation calling for?" Default to *empathic concern* in feedback and 360 reviews.
- Audit one job description per week for skills-vs-credentials drift. Replace "degree required" with the underlying capability and test for it.
- Run a good-riddance review: ask employees to nominate "anything poorly designed, unnecessary, or just plain stupid" (Hawaii Pacific Health's GROSS programme). Default Outlook meetings from 30 to 15 minutes (AstraZeneca saved thousands of hours).
- Use the four-freedoms audit to test DEI initiatives: which freedoms are unevenly distributed in your team, and which structural changes (policy, feedback, flexible work) would close that gap?
- For partners and senior ICs in client-facing work, screen for Activator behaviours, not just expertise.
- Define which version of stakeholder capitalism your board signs up to before the next public statement — instrumental, classic, beneficial, or structural.
- Once a year, run the seven-step life strategy on yourself: define a great life, purpose, vision, 16 SLU portfolio audit, benchmarks, choices, OKRs.

## Frameworks Extracted

- **Five paradigm shifts in reskilling (Tamayo et al.):** reskilling as strategic imperative; shared responsibility across managers; change-management not training; employee-centred design; ecosystem partnerships.
- **Rate–Direction–Method (RDM) framework (Pisano):** integrate target growth rate, sources of demand, and method (organic / acquire / partner / license) as one decision rather than three.
- **Sustainable Empathy (Zaki):** (1) Physician, heal thyself — self-compassion as prerequisite; (2) Tune your caring — distinguish emotional empathy from empathic concern; (3) Treat empathy as a skill to develop.
- **Skills-First Hiring (Ammerman, Groysberg, Rometty):** skills taxonomy → broaden talent pool → reimagine existing relationships → retrain managers → scale in cohorts → support with skills-based culture.
- **Friction Project — Good-Riddance Review + Subtraction Tools (Sutton & Rao):** simple subtraction rules; rule of halves; subtraction game; subtraction specialists ("jargon monoxide" remover); subtraction networks; subtraction movements (AstraZeneca's million-hour challenge).
- **Four Freedoms at Work (Roberts):** freedom to be; freedom to become; freedom to fade; freedom to fail. Each tied to specific interventions (antidiscrimination policy, strengths-based development, flexible work, psychological safety).
- **Activator Playbook (Dixon, McKenna, Channer, Freeman):** Commit (reserve weekly time for business development), Connect (curate a wide network across client and firm), Create (proactively bring insight, not wait for a brief). Activators outperform Experts/Confidants/Debaters/Realists.
- **Four versions of stakeholder capitalism (Paine):** instrumental (long-term shareholder value), classic (respect legitimate claims), beneficial (improve all stakeholders' wellbeing), structural (give stakeholders formal governance powers).
- **Sustainable Products: Independence / Dissonance / Resonance (Dalsace & Challagalla):** how sustainability interacts with the product's core benefit, mapped onto green / blue / gray customer segments.
- **Strategize Your Life (Strack, Dyrchs, Bailey):** 7 steps — define a great life (PERMA-V), purpose, vision, life portfolio across 6 SLAs / 16 SLUs, benchmarks, choices, OKRs anchored to consequences and check-ins.

## Contradictions / Tensions With Existing Wiki

- **Empathy as skill vs Lopp's stoic posture.** Zaki's "empathy is a skill to tune" complements [[principles/trust]] and [[management/one-on-ones]] but pushes back on the tacit assumption that strong leaders simply absorb others' distress. The wiki should adopt the *empathic concern vs emotional empathy* distinction.
- **Hiring on credentials vs Lopp's "hiring is the highest-leverage activity".** The HBR 2025 piece argues credentials are noisy filters that exclude capable talent. This refines [[management/hiring]] without contradicting it — the page's emphasis on culture-fit and pipeline still holds, but credential filtering needs an explicit warning.
- **Growth strategy vs Sinek's WHY.** Pisano's RDM frames growth as a deliberate constraint problem (you may need to grow slower than the market allows). This tensions with founder-mode "scale your WHY" framing in [[principles/purpose-and-why]] — both can be true, but the constraint side is missing in the wiki.
- **Subtraction vs management literature's bias toward addition.** Sutton & Rao's friction-as-default belongs alongside [[organization/operating-rhythm]] as a contradicting force: most operating rhythms accrete meetings rather than remove them.

## Questions This Source Raises

- How do the four freedoms apply in a small team with no formal DEI infrastructure? Roberts assumes a corporate scale.
- Can the Activator profile work for technical leaders who aren't doing sales? The behaviours (commit / connect / create) generalise, but the rewards structure does not.
- Where is the line between "good-riddance" and chesterton's-fence cargo cult removal? Sutton & Rao gesture at "good friction" but the boundary is fuzzy.
- For founders specifically, does Pisano's RDM advice ("pace growth to your bottleneck") apply, or does the venture-backed game force a different posture?
