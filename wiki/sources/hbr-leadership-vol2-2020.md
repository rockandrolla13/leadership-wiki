# HBR's 10 Must Reads on Leadership, Vol. 2

- **Author:** Multiple authors (Harvard Business Review)

- **Type:** Book (essay collection)
- **Path:** raw/books/markdown/HBRs 10 Must Reads on Leadership, Vol. 2.md

## Core Argument

A curated collection of HBR's most significant leadership articles, covering the full arc of modern leadership: from the shift from functional to enterprise leadership (Watkins), to organizational communication (Groysberg & Slind), to strategic leadership skills (Schoemaker et al.), to authenticity in transitions (Ibarra), to paradoxical leadership (Smith, Lewis, Tushman), to collaborative leadership (Ibarra & Hansen), to attention management for leaders (Goleman, bonus article). The collective argument: effective modern leadership requires moving beyond command-and-control, developing strategic mindsets, and building conversational and collaborative organizations.

## Key Leadership Insights

**Leadership Is a Conversation (Groysberg & Slind)**
- Command-and-control communication is no longer sufficient. Effective leaders engage in genuine conversation: intimate (get close to people), interactive (promote dialogue), inclusive (expand employee voice), intentional (pursue a coherent agenda).
- The conversational model lets large organizations function with the engagement of small ones.
- One-way broadcast is a relic; people will use their public voice regardless.

**How Managers Become Leaders — Seven Seismic Shifts (Watkins)**
- The transition from functional leader to enterprise leader requires seven distinct mindset shifts: Specialist → Generalist, Analyst → Integrator, Tactician → Strategist, Bricklayer → Architect, Problem Solver → Agenda Setter, Warrior → Diplomat, Supporting Cast → Lead Role.
- The skills that earned the promotion are not the skills needed for the new role.
- Common failure: defaulting to the function you know and undermanaging everything else.

**Strategic Leadership: Essential Skills (Schoemaker, Krupp, Howland)**
- Six skills for navigating uncertainty: Anticipate (scan for weak signals), Challenge (question assumptions), Interpret (synthesize ambiguous data), Decide (commit with incomplete information), Align (build stakeholder buy-in), Learn (extract lessons from outcomes).
- These skills cannot compensate for one another — strength in one does not offset weakness in another.
- Strategic leaders must develop all six; most executives are weak in at least two.

**The Authenticity Paradox (Ibarra)**
- Treating authenticity as "always behaving naturally" is a trap. Careers require moving outside comfort zones.
- "Adaptively authentic" is the right frame: try new behaviors, observe results, keep what works. This is growth, not fakery.
- Danger of "true-to-selfers": they use authenticity as an excuse to avoid behavior the role requires.
- Identity is "an evolving story" — update the narrative as the role demands, not as the past dictates.

**"Both/And" Leadership (Smith, Lewis, Tushman)**
- Strategic paradoxes cannot be resolved — they must be managed. Innovation vs. efficiency, global vs. local, profit vs. mission.
- The wrong response: pick a side and be "consistent." The right response: embrace the tension and manage dynamic equilibrium.
- Separate opposing goals into distinct units/metrics/processes to respect each; then connect them through higher purpose and bridging roles.

**Are You a Collaborative Leader? (Ibarra & Hansen)**
- Four elements of collaborative leadership: playing global connector (span boundaries), attracting diverse talent (not homogeneous networks), modeling collaboration at the top (the senior team demonstrates it), showing a strong hand (prevent consensus paralysis).
- Collaborative leaders are connectors — they bridge people and ideas that would not otherwise meet.

**The Focused Leader (Goleman, bonus)**
- Leaders face three types of focus: inner (self-awareness, managing internal states), other (empathy, reading people), and outer (strategic environment awareness).
- Attention is the foundation of all leadership competencies; without selective focus, decisions degrade.
- Triple focus — self, others, system — is the cognitive model of effective leadership.

## Practical Applications

- In every leadership transition: identify which of the seven seismic shifts you're struggling with most and invest in it deliberately.
- In organizational communication: assess whether your communication is intimate (close), interactive (two-way), inclusive (employs broader voice), and intentional (agenda-aligned).
- In strategic work: assess your six skills annually. Address your weakest before your next major strategic challenge.
- When facing strategic tensions: explicitly name the paradox. Resist choosing sides. Ask: "How can we do both?"
- When building collaborative leadership: become a connector — attend cross-industry events, blog outward-looking insights, build a diverse external network.

## Frameworks Extracted

- **Four Elements of Organizational Conversation:** Intimacy, Interactivity, Inclusion, Intentionality (Groysberg & Slind)
- **Seven Seismic Shifts:** Specialist → Generalist, Analyst → Integrator, Tactician → Strategist, Bricklayer → Architect, Problem Solver → Agenda Setter, Warrior → Diplomat, Supporting Cast → Lead (Watkins)
- **Six Strategic Leadership Skills:** Anticipate, Challenge, Interpret, Decide, Align, Learn (Schoemaker et al.)
- **Adaptively Authentic Leadership:** Treating identity as an evolving story; experimenting with new behaviors rather than defending a fixed self (Ibarra)
- **Both/And Paradoxical Leadership:** Separate (respect distinct demands) + Connect (build overarching purpose) to achieve dynamic equilibrium (Smith, Lewis, Tushman)
- **Triple Focus:** Inner (self), Other (empathy), Outer (system/environment) (Goleman)

## Contradictions / Tensions With Existing Wiki

- Ibarra's "adaptively authentic" framework could tension with Sinek's WHY emphasis on finding your authentic core purpose. Ibarra argues you should experiment with behaviors outside your natural style; Sinek argues your WHY is fixed and true. Possible resolution: the WHY (values, purpose) can be fixed while the HOW (style, behavior) adapts. Worth tracking.
